Fluids_MediaPlayer - patch notes: now-playing overlay
=====================================================

WHAT THIS PATCH DOES

  1. Position memory. The overlay remembers where you put it across restarts and when you
     swap to another wallpaper and back.
       - Fixed a bug where Lively delivering your settings late (slow boot, wallpaper swap)
         made the overlay forget a dragged position.
       - New setting "Saved position code" (overlay section, under Unlock). Drag the card with
         Unlock ticked, then paste the code shown under it into that box. Lively keeps that
         setting, so the spot survives restarts and swaps. Clear the box to put the card back
         in the default spot (bottom left of the first screen).

  2. Cleanup. The "Start on monitor" and "Start position" presets are gone; dragging the card
     (and the saved position code) replaces them.

  3. A little more space between the song title and the artist. The artist line stays where it
     was; only the title moves up. To change the amount, edit TITLE_GAP at the top of
     js/overlay-frame.js (0.3 = 30% of the title's font size; 0 = the old tight spacing).

LIVELY HAS TO BE RUNNING (this part is on you)
This patch can only REMEMBER the overlay's position. It cannot start Lively. If Lively is
not running there is no wallpaper at all, so nothing for the overlay to appear on.
Whether Lively starts with the PC is entirely up to the user.

We recommend adding it to your startup apps, but it is not required:
  - In Lively: Settings (gear) > General > turn on "Start with Windows"
  - or in Windows: Settings > Apps > Startup > turn on Lively Wallpaper
If you would rather launch Lively yourself each time, that works fine too. The saved
position is applied whenever the wallpaper starts.

-----------------------------------------------------
Per-monitor wave size + firework bursts
-----------------------------------------------------
1. Waves (and trail travel) now scale to each monitor's own height. Fill in "Monitor layout", e.g.
     4096x2160@200, 1920x1080@100
   (left to right). The script checks whether Lively gave it a canvas in real pixels or Windows-scaled
   units and picks the matching math. New slider "Wave size follows each monitor's size" (0 = off).
   Auto-detect can only guess the monitor COUNT, so mixed-size monitors need the layout filled in.
2. New slider "Firework bursts" (0 = old behaviour). Bursts spread away from recent ones, strong hits open
   in a small ring, and in intense parts bursts are a bit smaller, trails shorter and old dye fades faster.
   Fine tuning lives in INSTR.FW near the top of js/script.js.

3. "Waves start on" dropdown (Left monitor by default). Hits start on that monitor as a small bloom, cross to the
   other monitor as a tight streak, and spread out (big bloom + ring) where they land. Cross time per note is in
   INSTR.SHOT.T near the top of js/script.js. Nothing spawns outside a monitor's own area.
4. "Room off-screen (%)" slider (15 by default, 0 = off). The fluid area is bigger than the screen on every side, so
   waves drift off the edges instead of piling up. It costs GPU (resolution grows with it), so lower it if you
   see slowdown.
5. America mode (checkbox, on by default). When the art's only real colors are red and blue, the fluid is rebuilt as a flag:
   red 41.5% / white 36.9% / blue 21.5%, the proportions of the real U.S. flag (13 stripes, canton 7 stripes tall and 40% of the
   width, stars NOT counted so the canton stays solid blue and the fluid doesn't wash out). All reds merge into one red and all
   blues into one blue; cream / faded-white stripes become white instead of a vivid yellow, and white is added if there were none.
   The red and blue are locked to flag hues (AMERICA.RED_OUT / BLUE_OUT) so tints in the art (orange, cyan text) can't shift them.
   The primary-color lean and instrument role colors are skipped so the flag mix stays exact. Art with a true yellow (yellow + black,
   red + yellow + blue) is untouched. Settings are in AMERICA near the palette code.
6. Color detection rework (grayscale mask). Before anything is decided, every black and grayscale pixel (black, gray, white) is thrown
   away completely (ART.MIN_SAT). Everything after that - which colors to pick, whether the art has color at all - only sees the pixels
   that survived. This fixes covers that are mostly black with a thin band of color (Dark Side of the Moon's rainbow) falling back to
   red + rose, and muted / faded covers (a washed-out flag) doing the same. The old share-of-the-whole-cover tests are gone. Slightly
   tinted neutrals (cream, beige, sepia) survive the mask but score low, so they only matter when nothing stronger is there. White only
   exists in America mode. One noise floor remains (ART.MIN_COLOR_SHARE, 0.2%): a speck or compression noise on black & white art
   still counts as black & white. Art is also sampled at 128 px instead of 64 so thin bands keep their true color.
7. Wave colors follow the cover's color ratio. After the grayscale mask, each picked color's weight is its share of the surviving
   (colored) pixels; hues that weren't picked (too close to a picked hue, too small, or past 8 colors) give their area to the nearest
   picked hue so the shares add up. The old flattening (WEIGHT_FLATTEN / MULTI_FLATTEN, which boosted small accents) is removed.
   Two-color art uses its own ratio instead of the 'Two-color balance' slider (the slider still drives your own two colors).
   NOTE: the 'Primary color' option still pushes the biggest color on top of this; turn it off for the pure ratio.
8. Color rotation for art with 3+ colors. Pure area ratio washed busy covers out (a big cream / yellow area swamped everything else),
   so 3+ color art now uses a loop: primary, secondary, next of the remaining colors, primary, secondary, next of the remaining, ...
   Primary and secondary each get 1/3 of the splats, the other colors share the last 1/3 and are walked through in order.
   Importance = area x vividness (faint cream counts little), not raw area. Applies to instrument hits and random / burst splats.
   1-2 color art and America mode are unchanged. The Primary color option no longer affects 3+ color art (the rotation decides).
   Switch: ART.ROTATE_MIN_COLORS (3).
9. Yellow is never the primary color. If the art has yellow (hue 45-70 degrees, ART.YELLOW_HUE) it automatically takes the secondary
   slot; the best non-yellow color is primary and the rest follow in rank order. Yellow still plays in the loop. A yellow-only cover
   has nothing else to lead with, so it is unchanged.
10. Off-white is dismissed as white. A pixel that is bright (ART.WHITE_MIN_VAL, 0.80) and barely saturated (ART.WHITE_MAX_SAT 0.20, or
    ART.WARM_WHITE_MAX_SAT 0.30 for cream / ivory hues) is closer to white than to any color, so it is masked out with the grayscale.
    This stops a cream icon on a one-color cover from turning into a vivid yellow. Single-color art now always gets the same hue in a
    darker tone as its partner (ART.PARTNER_VAL), never a lighter or different color.
11. Stray greens fixed. Two filters: (a) anti-aliasing blends are ignored (a pixel on the straight line between two clearly different
    neighbors AND muddier than them, e.g. the green fringe where yellow meets blue; ART.BLEND_*), and (b) a hue must cover at least
    0.5% of the colored pixels to become a wave color, smaller slivers hand their area to the nearest picked hue (ART.MIN_AREA_SHARE).
12. America mode: instruments keep their own flag color again instead of picking by ratio (the old pick put white on 37% of every
    instrument's hits). Red = drums + bass (they fire the most), white = vocals, blue = track + guitar (AMERICA.ROLE_COLOR). The ratio still sets the weights of random splats.
13. Fireplace mode (checkbox, on by default). Art whose only real colors are yellow and / or orange (hue 12-70 degrees) becomes fire:
    each wave is born hot yellow and cools to orange, then deep red as it travels (trail puffs are colored by the wave's age, random
    splats sit anywhere along that range). The orange comes from the art when it has one, otherwise a stock orange; the yellow and red are
    added when missing. The art must also be bright and vivid (FIRE.MIN_VAL / MIN_SAT, 0.70) so brown, rust and wood tones are not fire. Settings in FIRE near the palette code. America mode is checked first; art with red / blue / green is not fire.
14. New slider 'America mode: white on drums + bass' (default 20%). That share of drum and bass hits plays white instead of red, so the
    flag's white can be brought in on the busiest instruments without giving up red. 0 = all red. Code: AMERICA.WHITE_SUB / WHITE_SUB_ROLES.
15. Fireplace mode reworked so the fire shifts in real time. Detection: art whose only real colors are red, orange and / or yellow (bright and
    vivid) is fire, and whichever of the three is missing is added. Waves run on a heat scale (0 yellow, 0.5 orange, 1 red): each new wave is born
    at a heat that drifts slowly back and forth over time (FIRE.PERIOD, 30 s) with some spread (FIRE.SPREAD) so all three colors stay around, and
    it cools toward red along its trail. How fast it cools follows the wave's speed: slow waves (bass) are a slow burn that stays warm
    (FIRE.COOL_SLOW), fast waves (highs, hard hits) are sparks that flash through every color (FIRE.COOL_FAST). Before, every wave was born hot yellow and only the cursor showed other colors.

-----------------------------------------------------
Fireplace: yellow limit
-----------------------------------------------------
New slider "Fireplace: yellow limit" (70 by default, 0 = the old look). Yellow is the brightest of the fire's three
colors and the fluid adds colors together, so it washed the fire out, especially at the hot end of the 30 second heat
drift. fireGate() in js/script.js now (1) raises the hottest allowed heat so the top end is amber gold instead of lemon,
(2) bends births toward orange / red, (3) caps how much yellow can be out at once, and (4) dims the yellow end.
Knobs are in the FIRE block (FLOOR, CURVE, YELLOW_CAP, HOT_DIM).

-----------------------------------------------------
Neon boost
-----------------------------------------------------
New slider "Neon boost" (70 by default, 0 = off). Neon art (fluorescent yellow, hot cyan / pink) was washing out in the
fluid: thin dye looked like dark khaki, overlapping colors added up to white, and the bloom pushed bright cores toward
white. When most of the album's color is neon (very saturated AND bright, see NEON in js/script.js), the display shader
now fades the thin haze back to dark, restores saturation, and keeps overexposed cores in their color instead of clipping
to white. It eases in and out with the song, and ordinary art, fire and flag palettes are not touched.

-----------------------------------------------------
Neon boost, v2 (spectrum-aware haze)
-----------------------------------------------------
The first neon pass fixed the cores but left a mustard fog on yellow albums. Cause: yellow and cyan carry far more brightness per
bit of dye than red or blue, so a thin film of them fills the screen as a dull fog (a dim yellow is mustard, a dim red is just a
deeper red). The neon pass now runs AFTER the bloom is added (so the glow can't bring the fog back) and crushes the faint haze of
bright hues toward dark, leaving them as glowing shapes on a dark ground. Red / blue haze is left alone, so albums like
Samurai keep their soft look. Same "Neon boost" slider.
